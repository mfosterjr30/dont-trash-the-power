Know your batteries

Different batteries.
Different destinations.

Use the battery label and the device it came from to identify what you have. Then confirm the exact type with the receiving program.

🔋

Lithium-ion (Li-ion)

Rechargeable packs in phones, laptops, cordless tools, tablets, and many portable devices. Do not place in trash or curbside recycling.

RechargeableSpecialized drop-off
🪫

Nickel-based (Ni-Cd / Ni-MH / Ni-Zn)

Rechargeable batteries found in older devices, cordless tools, cameras, and some household electronics. Use a program that accepts the chemistry.

RechargeableProgram rules vary
🟠

Button & coin cells

Small round batteries used in watches, remotes, hearing aids, and devices. Keep away from children; tape or bag as directed by the recycler.

Single-use or rechargeable
🔌

Alkaline & zinc-carbon

Common AA, AAA, C, D, and 9V household batteries. Options differ by local rules; check your city or county program and retailer guidance.

Single-useLocal rules vary
🚗

Lead-acid batteries

Common in cars, motorcycles, boats, and some backup systems. Return through an auto-battery retailer or an approved hazardous-waste program.

Heavy / corrosiveDedicated return
🛴

E-bike & large packs

Large-format lithium batteries require dedicated handling. Contact the manufacturer, dealer, or a program specifically equipped to accept them.

Large formatCall ahead
Do not assume acceptance. A collection box may accept only certain rechargeable batteries, sizes, weights, or conditions. The list above is educational—not an event acceptance list.
