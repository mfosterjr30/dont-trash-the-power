Learn the risks

Battery safety
starts before disposal.

Used batteries are not all alike. A few simple habits can reduce fire risks and help keep batteries out of the wrong waste stream.

🔥

Fire risk

Lithium-ion batteries can ignite if damaged, crushed, short-circuited, or improperly handled during collection and processing.

♻️

Wrong-bin risk

Household recycling facilities are generally not designed to receive loose batteries. Follow local instructions and use specialized programs.

🔋

Resource loss

Discarded batteries can contain materials that specialized recycling processes may recover for future use.

At home

Store batteries with care.

  • Keep used batteries in a cool, dry place away from children and pets.
  • Keep batteries away from metal objects that could bridge terminals.
  • Follow the recycler's instructions for taping terminals or individual bagging.
  • Keep different battery types separated when the program requires it.
  • Do not crush, puncture, dismantle, or intentionally damage batteries.
Important safety warning

Damaged, swollen, hot, leaking, or recalled batteries need special handling.

Do not place them in a public collection box or ordinary drop-off bin. Keep people away, avoid handling them, and contact your local household hazardous waste program or fire department for instructions. If a battery is smoking, hissing, or on fire, move away and call 911.

The takeaway

Never guess. Check first.

Battery chemistry, condition, and local rules matter. Verify acceptance with the collection site before transporting batteries.
